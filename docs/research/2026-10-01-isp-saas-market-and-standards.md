# ISP SaaS — market and standards research (2026-10-01)

Two desk-research passes (search-snippet depth; vendor pages are biased and some sources were paywalled or blocked). `[V]` = read in a source, `[U]` = unverified/background. Treat as directional input to spec §12, not as verified fact.

## Market

- **Billing/OSS suites:** Splynx (strong in Africa; billing, RADIUS, CRM, white-label app, TR-069 ACS add-on, hotspot/voucher, Netcash integration) [V]; Sonar (North America; no SA rails) [V]; Powercode, UISP/UCRM, Azotel [U]. Priced per active subscriber (about $0.165–$1.25 per month) [V].
- **Hotspot billing/monetisation (closest to Sentech's hotspot use case):** XenFi (SA, 3% per sale, QR voucher batches, agent cash, MikroTik/UniFi/Omada) [V]; SopraEnt (flat $7/month per ISP, branded subdomain, isolated data, agent commissions) [V]; Powerlynx, Hotwireless, Antamedia [V, vendor blog]; Purple, Tanaza, Cloud4Wi [U].
- **MVNO/MVNE platforms:** Gigs, Telgoo5, Optiva, Cerillion (used by MVNX in SA), mvne.co.za; Lago is an open-source usage-billing option (AGPLv3) [U].
- **White-label apps:** Splynx includes a logo/icon-branded app; store rules make true white-label awkward (one store account per brand, or a shared app with runtime theming) [U].
- **SA specifics:** Fibertime (R5/day, vouchers at tills), Vumatel Vuma Key (EasyPay retail, 90-day recharge) [V]; 1Voucher/Blu Voucher/EasyPay cash rails; Netcash, PayFast, Peach, Ozow, Stitch, Yoco gateways [U]; AlwaysOn and Liquid roaming hubs [U].
- **Table stakes seen:** customers/plans, prepaid and postpaid, vouchers with batches and agent inventory, RADIUS with CoA and accounting, payment reconciliation, reseller hierarchy with commissions, per-ISP branded portal with isolated data, customer app, reporting, API/webhooks, audit and export.
- **Commercial models:** per active subscriber; per site/AP/concurrent user; 1.5–3% transaction take-rate; small flat fee. No public detail was found on platform-collected payments with split settlement between wholesaler, reseller and ISP, or on roaming settlement tariffs.
- **Pitfalls:** missing tenant id in a query (broken object-level authorisation); multi-site RADIUS without a shared session store; load-shedding reconnect storms; app-store white-label rules; unclear RICA treatment of anonymous voucher sessions; township cash/voucher channels.
- **Gaps Sentech can fill:** consumer-facing cross-ISP compare-and-switch; one app/storefront/admin across hotspot passes and subscriptions; wholesale-aware settlement ledger; first-class SA rails; white-label beyond logo/icon; documented tenant isolation and audit; load-shedding-resilient accounting; subscriber/voucher import from Splynx, Mikhmon and MikroTik User Manager.

## Standards and best practice (what they require of the design)

- **Multi-tenant SaaS (AWS SaaS Lens, Microsoft multitenant guidance, OWASP):** tenant identity bound to user identity and carried in every layer; authn/authz is not isolation, so RLS is a separate layer from RBAC; automated repeatable onboarding/offboarding; tenant-aware metrics, logs and cost; per-tenant config instead of forks; tenant lifecycle states (trial, active, suspended, merged/split, offboarded, reactivated); deployment rings and feature flags; metering from an accurate store; a tenant→deployment mapping so a large ISP can later move to a dedicated DB/stamp.
- **Postgres RLS pitfalls:** owners/superusers/BYPASSRLS bypass policies (use a non-owner runtime role, FORCE where needed); session variables can break under pooling (use transaction-scoped settings and test connection reuse across tenants); FK/unique checks are a covert channel; non-leakproof functions in policies can leak; use WITH CHECK on writes; include tenant in cache keys; a CI test that every tenant table has RLS; client-supplied tenant ids are selectors only. Global tables (directory, migration hand-offs) belong in a separate schema behind audited SECURITY DEFINER functions.
- **Identity:** prefer one Keycloak realm for end customers with Organizations as ISPs and staff RBAC/delegation in the app DB; reserve dedicated realms for silo/enterprise-IdP tenants; Organizations was a technology preview in Keycloak 25 (check status in the version used); audit impersonation with impersonator and subject ids.
- **White-label:** Apple guideline 4.2.6 rejects template/app-generation-service apps unless submitted by the content provider and allows a single aggregated "picker" app; 4.3 bans multiple bundle ids of the same app; 5.1.1(v) requires in-app account deletion. Launch with one app + tenant picker; per-ISP builds later from each ISP's own developer account. Per-tenant design tokens as remote config, per-tenant domains with automated TLS, versioned per-tenant legal documents with acceptance records.
- **Roaming/federation:** OpenRoaming (RadSec/mTLS, NAPTR discovery, settled vs settlement-free RCOIs, mandatory accounting, rotating CUI), RFC 6614 RadSec and RFC 7585 discovery (both Experimental), RFC 5176 CoA/Disconnect (UDP/MD5; use only via an authenticated proxy path), eduroam and Passpoint as reference federations. Minimal safe path: no roaming at launch; tenant-scoped RADIUS realms `user@isp-slug`; later a platform-run RADIUS proxy with static bilateral peers, CDRs for every roaming session and an immutable double-entry settlement ledger; OpenRoaming/Passpoint membership after that. Cross-ISP migration: customer-initiated, consent-stamped, minimal transfer package, saga with audit, no raw cross-tenant reads.
- **TM Forum:** adopt TMF data-model vocabulary and a thin facade later (TMF620/622 catalog and ordering; TMF666/678 accounts and bills; TMF629/632/676 party/customer/payment; TMF668 partnership for reseller/wholesale); full ODA certification and eTOM mapping are overkill at launch.
- **Security/compliance:** OWASP ASVS 5.0 (target L2, L3 for auth, RADIUS-secret, key and support-tooling paths) plus the OWASP Multi-Tenant cheat sheet; SOC 2 / ISO 27001 evidence trail from the start. POPIA: each ISP is the responsible party and the platform the operator (written operator agreement, s19–22 security, immediate breach notification to the responsible party); s72 cross-border transfers (watch non-SA sub-processors); Regulator breach portal (from April 2025); s69 direct marketing. The directory and migration services may make Sentech a responsible party for that processing (counsel). RICA s39: per-tenant data and retention; Chapter 7 is under review. ICASA: licence or exemption obligations sit with each ISP; gate onboarding on a licence/exemption number. PCI: prefer full-redirect payments (Netcash, PCI Level 1; SAQ A). **Open legal question:** is each ISP the merchant of record (funds direct to the ISP) or does Sentech collect on their behalf (payment-facilitator / trust-account / FICA issues)? CPA/ECTA: per-tenant supplier disclosures and cooling-off.
- **Operations:** per-component SLOs with RADIUS auth availability as its own SLO; status page; tenant id on every log/metric/trace; time-boxed reason-coded impersonation with dual-id audit; tenant-visible audit logs; per-tenant version tracking and an emergency-patch policy; versioned public API with deprecation windows and HMAC-signed webhooks; per-tenant rate limits; self-service export and offboarding with retention then crypto-shred; per-tenant envelope encryption for PII and RICA documents.

## Top risks

1. Cross-tenant leak from an RLS gap. 2. Platform role ambiguity under POPIA (directory, migration, roaming). 3. Commingling of funds / merchant-of-record status. 4. Tenant ISPs failing RICA/ICASA obligations. 5. App-store rejection of white-label apps. 6. Keycloak Organizations/realm misfit. 7. Roaming abuse and settlement disputes. 8. Noisy neighbour (RADIUS accounting bursts, heavy reports). 9. Support-tool/impersonation abuse. 10. Draft/experimental standards shifting and compliance-evidence debt.
